Governing Equations

1. Water mass balance

dS/dt = Q_supply(t) − Q_demand(t)

S = strategic storage volume (m³); Q in m³/day.

2. Days of strategic storage autonomy

D_cov = S / Q_demand

Matches KAHRAMAA's published definition (e.g. "5.2 days" in 2024) — how long strategic reservoirs alone could cover national demand if all active supply stopped today. Independent of the current supply/demand balance, so it never shows an undefined "∞" value.

3. Desalination power demand

P (kW) = SEC (kWh/m³) × Q (m³/day) / 24

4. Associated grid emissions

E_CO2 (kg/day) = SEC × Q × EF_grid  [EF_grid ≈ 0.58 kgCO₂/kWh]

5. Blended levelized cost of water

LCOW_blend = Σ(Qᵢ·LCOWᵢ) / ΣQᵢ

6. Per-capita consumption gap

PCC (L/day) = Q_domestic × 1000 / Population

7. Deficit closure time (crisis mode)

T_close = max( deployment_time_i ) over activated alternatives i, subject to Σgain_i ≥ Deficit

Methodology Notes

  • National totals (desal capacity, demand, storage, TSE reuse) use the latest public KAHRAMAA / Ashghal disclosures.
  • Plant capacities: RAF A3 = 164,000 m³/day and Umm Al Houl = 614,000 m³/day are validated project figures (ACCIONA; Qatar University, 2022). Ras Laffan and the residual fleet are indicative allocations calibrated to match the validated 538 MIGD (2024) national total.
  • SEC (specific energy consumption) values are indicative engineering-literature benchmarks by technology (modern SWRO ≈3.5–4.0 kWh/m³; older thermal MSF/MED ≈8–12+ kWh/m³ equivalent; groundwater pumping ≈0.4 kWh/m³; TSE treatment+pumping ≈0.6 kWh/m³) — not plant-specific KAHRAMAA metering data, which is not publicly published at this granularity.
  • Grid emission factor ≈0.58 kgCO₂/kWh reflects Qatar's ~96% gas-fired generation mix (WRI Global Power Plant Database-based estimate, 2025).
  • Alternative-response costs (QR/m³) and deployment times are planning-level indicative bands drawn from international desalination/water-reuse cost literature, scaled to Qatar's context — not official Kahramaa/Ashghal tariffs.
  • Municipal demand allocation is broken down into four end-use sectors — domestic/drinking, landscape & public irrigation, agriculture, and industrial/district-cooling — using population share for the domestic+landscape pool and node-specific loadings for agriculture and industry (indicative; see per-node sector split on the Network tab).
  • Groundwater well fields shown on the map are natural aquifer sources accessed via well-field pumping (not treated/reused water); they are separate from TSE (treated sewage effluent) reuse sites.
